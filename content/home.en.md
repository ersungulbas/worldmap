## A free map of the countries you've visited

worldmap.tech turns "how many countries have you been to?" into a map you can see, save and share. Tap each country you've visited and it lights up. Your count, your share of the world and your traveler level update as you go. There is no account, no app to install and no email to give. The map above is the whole tool, and everything you mark stays in your own browser.

## How to use it

- **Mark your countries.** Tap a country on the map to mark it, and tap it again to remove it. Small places such as Andorra, Singapore or the Caribbean islands are easier to find with the search box at the top.
- **Watch your numbers.** The counter at the bottom shows how many countries you've marked and what percentage of the world that is.
- **Save an image.** Press Save and pick Story, Square or Wide. The image is drawn on your device and can include a QR code that opens your map.
- **Share and compare.** Send your link to a friend. When they open it, both maps appear together and the countries you've both visited turn purple.
- **Keep it safe.** Your map lives in this browser. To move it to another phone or computer, open your link there, or ask for an anonymous 8-character restore code.

## How your % of the world is counted

The map has 233 selectable places, including territories such as Greenland, Hong Kong and Puerto Rico. Your percentage, though, is always counted out of 195: the 193 UN member states plus Vatican City and Palestine. That keeps the number comparable with the lists most travelers use. So 20 countries is about 10% of the world, and 39 countries is exactly 20%.

## Traveler levels

Your level grows with your count: Curious Traveler from your first country, Wanderer at 5, Explorer at 10, Globetrotter at 20, Passport Pro at 35, Travel Legend at 60 and Citizen of the World at 100. It is a light way to see how far you've come and what the next milestone looks like.

## What counts as a visit?

That is your call, and there is no single right answer. Some travelers count an airport transfer, others only count a country once they've spent a night there or left the airport. Whatever rule you pick, using the same rule for every country is what makes your map honest.
