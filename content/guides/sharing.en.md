The simplest way to share your travel map is to do two things: save it as an image in the format that fits where you're posting, and send your share link to anyone who should be able to open the real map. The image is for looking at; the link is for exploring and comparing. Everything below builds on that pair, from choosing a format to yearly before/after posts and a few privacy habits worth keeping.

## Pick the format for where it's going

When you tap Save, you choose between three sizes. Each one shows your country count, your traveler level and your % of the world, so the numbers travel with the picture.

- **Story (1080×1920):** tall and full-screen, made for Instagram and WhatsApp stories.
- **Square (1080×1080):** the classic shape for a feed post.
- **Wide (1920×1080):** a desktop wallpaper, a slide in a presentation, a post on X/Twitter or a blog header.

The taller formats, Story and Square, also add the flags of the countries you've marked, which makes a long list easier to take in than a map full of tiny shapes. If most of your travels are in small countries, like a weekend in Luxembourg or a run of Caribbean islands, the flags do a lot of the work, because those places are hard to spot on a world map at phone size.

The image is made right on your own device. Before you save, give your map one last look: it's easy to forget a country you only crossed for a day. If you're not sure whether a quick stop counts, our guide on [what counts as visiting a country](/guides/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country/) can help you decide.

## Add a QR code, or just send the link

A picture is a snapshot; the link is the map itself. When you save, you can add a QR code to the image that opens your map. That's handy anywhere people can see your image but can't tap it: a slide in a talk about your gap year, a printout on the fridge, a screen at a family dinner.

The share link (worldmap.tech/#w…) contains your whole map, so nobody needs an account, neither you nor the person opening it. Because the countries live inside the link, it reflects your map at the moment you copied it. After a new trip, copy a fresh link and send that one.

## Compare maps with friends and family

When a friend opens your link, or you open theirs, both maps meet on one screen. Their countries show in orange, yours in blue, and the ones you've both visited in purple. It's a quick way to see who's been where, and it tends to start better conversations than a plain count does.

A few ways to use it:

- **Plan the next trip together.** Countries neither of you has marked are the obvious shortlist for a joint trip.
- **Ask for tips.** Orange countries are places your friend knows and you don't; purple ones are where you can swap stories and argue about the best food.
- **Compare across generations.** Parents and grandparents can have very different maps from their kids, and seeing them side by side is a nice way to hear the old travel stories.
- **Recap a group trip.** After traveling together, compare maps: the countries from that trip show up purple, along with any others you've both been to.

## Make a yearly before/after post

Travel maps change slowly, which is exactly why a yearly comparison is satisfying. Save an image on January 1, or on your birthday, and keep it somewhere safe. A year later, save a new one and post them side by side or as two story slides. The count, the % of the world and the level make the change easy to read even for people who don't know your trips.

Level changes make natural captions. Going from Explorer at 10 countries to Globetrotter at 20 is a real milestone, and the [traveler levels guide](/guides/traveler-levels/) explains what each step tends to take.

## Think about privacy before you post

A travel map says more than it looks like it does. Your home country is usually marked, and the pattern of nearby countries can hint at where you live. A few simple habits help:

**Post after you get home.** A map shared from a beach, with a new country freshly marked, tells everyone you're away right now. Waiting until you're back costs nothing.

**Decide who gets the QR code.** The QR code and the link open your interactive map for anyone who has them, and let them compare it with their own. If you'd rather keep that among friends, leave the QR code off public posts and send the link privately.

**Know what the app needs from you.** Nothing personal. There's no sign-up and no email; your map is saved in your browser, and the restore code is anonymous.

## Opening your map from Instagram or TikTok

Apps like Instagram and TikTok open links in their own built-in browser, and that browser keeps its storage separate from Safari or Chrome. If you mark countries there, you may not find them later in your usual browser. Open worldmap.tech in your normal browser to keep your map in one place. If you've already marked countries inside the app, bring them over with your share link or with the anonymous 8-character restore code, which moves your map to another browser or device without any email.
