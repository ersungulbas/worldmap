The world's ten smallest countries cover about 1,330 km² between them. That is less than Greater London, which covers roughly 1,570 km². On your map, though, each of them counts as a full country, just like Russia or Canada. This guide lists the ten smallest, shows where they are and how people get there.

## The 10 smallest countries by area

The ranking uses the list of 195: the 193 UN member states plus the observer states Vatican City and Palestine. Areas are approximate, and sources differ slightly depending on how they measure.

- **1. Vatican City:** about 0.44 km². Inside Rome, the smallest country in the world by both area and population.
- **2. Monaco:** about 2 km². On France's Mediterranean coast, the most densely populated country in the world.
- **3. Nauru:** about 21 km². A single-island republic in the Pacific with no official capital.
- **4. Tuvalu:** about 26 km². Nine small islands and atolls in the Pacific.
- **5. San Marino:** about 61 km². Surrounded by Italy, on the slopes of Mount Titano.
- **6. Liechtenstein:** about 160 km². In the Rhine valley between Switzerland and Austria.
- **7. Marshall Islands:** about 181 km². Coral atolls scattered across a vast stretch of the Pacific.
- **8. Saint Kitts and Nevis:** about 261 km². Two Caribbean islands, the smallest country in the Americas.
- **9. Maldives:** about 298 km². Nearly twelve hundred islands in the Indian Ocean, the smallest country in Asia.
- **10. Malta:** about 316 km². In the middle of the Mediterranean, the smallest member of the EU.

## Europe's five

Half the list is in Europe, and four of them are surprisingly close together.

**Vatican City** is a city-state inside Rome. You walk into St. Peter's Square and barely notice the border. The Vatican Museums and the Sistine Chapel are inside the country too. Fewer than a thousand people live there.

**Monaco** sits on France's Mediterranean coast, under half an hour from Nice by regional train. It covers about 2 km² but is home to around 38,000 people, which makes it the most densely populated country in the world.

**San Marino** is completely surrounded by Italy. By tradition it was founded in the year 301, and it presents itself as the world's oldest republic. There is no train; buses from Rimini on the Adriatic coast take about an hour.

**Liechtenstein** lies in the Rhine valley between Switzerland and Austria. It has no airport, and local buses connect it to train stations in Switzerland and Austria. Along with Uzbekistan, it is one of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world: a landlocked country surrounded only by landlocked countries.

**Malta** is made up of three inhabited islands south of Sicily and is the EU's smallest member. You get there by plane or by ferry from Sicily.

## The ocean micro-nations

The other five are island nations, and they take more effort to reach.

**Nauru** is a republic made up of a single coral island in the Pacific. It has no official capital; government offices are in the Yaren district. The road around the island is just under 20 kilometers long.

**Tuvalu** is made up of nine small islands and atolls, and its highest point is only a few meters above sea level. That puts it among the countries most exposed to rising seas. Flights usually go through Fiji.

**The Marshall Islands** are small in land but spread out in space: the country's land is split between 29 atolls and 5 islands scattered over a huge area of the Pacific.

**Saint Kitts and Nevis** is a two-island nation in the Caribbean and the smallest country in the Americas by both area and population.

**The Maldives** are made up of about 1,200 coral islands grouped into 26 atolls in the Indian Ocean. By average elevation, they are the lowest country in the world.

## Why they are hard to find on a map

On a world map, most of these countries are smaller than a dot. On a phone screen showing the whole world, Vatican City is far smaller than a single pixel. That is why, on worldmap.tech, the easiest way to mark them is the search box at the top right: type the name and mark it from the results. You can also zoom in, but searching is much quicker.

Small as they are, these countries add as much to your world % as big ones. The % uses 195, so Vatican City and Russia each count as one country. That makes Europe's small countries one of the easiest ways to add to your map: a trip to Rome can include the Vatican, and a stay in Nice can include Monaco. Whether a quick visit counts is your call; the guide to [what counts as a visit](/guides/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country/) covers microstates in particular.
