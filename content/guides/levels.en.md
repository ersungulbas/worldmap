Your traveler level on worldmap.tech depends on one thing: how many countries you've marked. You start as a Curious Traveler with your first country, then become a Wanderer at 5, an Explorer at 10, a Globetrotter at 20, a Passport Pro at 35, a Travel Legend at 60 and a Citizen of the World at 100. Next to your level sits your % of the world, which is your count divided by 195. Here is what each milestone tends to look like on a real map, how the math works, and some realistic ways to move up.

## The seven levels at a glance

The number in parentheses is the % of the world you'll see at that exact count.

- **1 country, Curious Traveler** (1%): your first country on the map, whether that's home or your first trip abroad.
- **5 countries, Wanderer** (3%): a handful of trips, or one well-planned one.
- **10 countries, Explorer** (5%): travel has become a habit rather than an event.
- **20 countries, Globetrotter** (10%): your map now spans more than one region.
- **35 countries, Passport Pro** (18%): you plan vacations around places you haven't been yet.
- **60 countries, Travel Legend** (31%): several continents, and some trips that took real effort.
- **100 countries, Citizen of the World** (51%): more than half of the 195.

When you cross a threshold, a "New level" message pops up on screen, and your level is printed on the image you save, so it travels with every map you share.

## What each milestone looks like in real life

**Getting to 5.** Five countries often means your neighbors plus one bigger trip, the kind that needed a flight and a proper plan. It can also happen in a single week if you pick a cluster of small countries that sit close together, which we cover in [how to visit many countries in one trip](/guides/visit-many-countries-in-one-trip/).

**Getting to 10.** At Explorer level, a map is usually still concentrated in one part of the world with an outlier or two: a long-haul vacation, a wedding abroad, a work trip that came with a free weekend.

**Getting to 20.** Globetrotter usually takes a couple of regions. Twenty could be a good chunk of Western Europe plus a trip around mainland Southeast Asia, or the Balkans, the Caucasus and a stopover in the Gulf. It's also exactly 10% of the world, a nice round number to put on a story.

**Getting to 35.** This is where people start choosing destinations partly because they're new. By now you've probably repeated the "several countries in one trip" trick a few times and spent time on at least two or three continents.

**Getting to 60.** Geography starts working against you here. Even by a broad definition, Europe has about 50 sovereign states, so you can't reach 60 on one continent's worth of easy train rides. Getting here usually means a long trip or two, plus a few destinations that needed extra planning.

**Getting to 100.** Citizen of the World is a long-term project. At 100 you've passed the halfway mark of 195, and the countries still missing tend to be the ones that take more effort to reach, with fewer flights and longer journeys.

## How your % of the world is calculated

The % is your country count divided by 195, rounded to the nearest whole number. The 195 is the 193 member states of the United Nations plus the two UN observer states, Vatican City and Palestine. It's a common answer to "how many countries are there?", and our guide on [how many countries there are](/guides/how-many-countries-are-there/) explains the other lists you might come across.

A few details worth knowing. Your first country already shows 1%, so the number moves from the very first tap. Thirty-nine countries is exactly 20%. And the % never goes above 100.

## Why your count can go past 195

The map has 233 selectable places, not 195. Some of the extra ones are places that feel like separate destinations when you're there, such as Hong Kong, Macau, Taiwan and Northern Cyprus, and others are territories like Greenland or Puerto Rico. Each one you tap is added to your count and helps you toward the next level. Your % still uses 195 as the base, so in theory a very dedicated traveler could pass 195 places while the % sits at 100.

## Realistic ways to reach the next level

You don't need a round-the-world ticket. Small changes to how you plan trips usually add more countries than extra trips do.

- **Combine neighbors.** Benelux, the Baltics and Central Europe each give you three or four countries in one trip, linked by train or bus rides of a few hours or less.
- **Go overland.** If you're flying into one country and out of another, look at what sits in between. A bus or train across a border turns one destination into two.
- **Add a day trip across a border.** Vienna and Bratislava are about an hour apart by train, and Monaco is a short train ride from Nice. If you're already there, it's an easy extra.
- **Use long layovers, if you count them.** A connection long enough to leave the airport and see the city can feel like a real visit. Whether an airport transit counts is your call; our guide on [what counts as visiting a country](/guides/what-counts-as-visiting-a-country/) covers the common rules.
- **Go back for the country you skipped.** If your map has a gap in the middle of a cluster you've already explored, filling it is usually easier than starting a new region from scratch.

Nobody checks your map but you, so set your own rules and stick to them. The level means more when you know exactly how you earned it.
