Sort the world's 195 countries by continent and Africa comes out on top: 54 countries on its own, more than a quarter of the total. Asia follows with 48 and Europe with 44. Those numbers can shift by a country or two, though, depending on how you define the continents and where you put the countries that sit on the line. This guide gives the count for each continent, the borderline cases, and a few ways to work through your own map one continent at a time.

## How many continents are there?

Even that question has more than one answer. The most common model has seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, South America and Oceania (sometimes called Australia). In most of Latin America and parts of Europe, the Americas are taught as a single continent, which brings the total down to six. The Olympic rings stand for five inhabited continents: Africa, the Americas, Asia, Europe and Oceania.

The split between Europe and Asia comes from history more than geography. Both are parts of one landmass, Eurasia, and the line between them usually follows the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian Sea, the Caucasus and the Turkish Straits. Because that line is far less obvious than an ocean, every list that sorts countries into continents has to make a few calls.

## Countries per continent

The numbers below use the list of 195: the 193 UN member states plus the two observer states, Vatican City and Palestine. To place countries, we followed the UN Statistics Division's M49 regions and split the Americas the usual way, with Central America and the Caribbean in North America. Most atlases and reference sources give the same breakdown.

- **Africa: 54 countries.** From Morocco and Egypt in the north to South Africa, plus Madagascar, Mauritius and Seychelles in the Indian Ocean.
- **Asia: 48 countries.** Including Türkiye, Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan in the Caucasus, the Middle East, Central Asia, India, China and Southeast Asia.
- **Europe: 44 countries.** Including Russia and Vatican City, from Iceland to Ukraine and from Malta to Norway.
- **North America: 23 countries.** Canada, the United States and Mexico, the seven countries of Central America and 13 island nations in the Caribbean.
- **Oceania: 14 countries.** Australia, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and the island nations of the Pacific.
- **South America: 12 countries.** The whole continent is divided between just twelve.
- **Antarctica: 0 countries.** No state has recognized territory there.

That adds up to 195. On a map, the colors fall like this.

## Countries on two continents

Some countries run straight across a continental line, and which continent they are filed under is partly a convention.

**Türkiye** is one of the best-known examples. About 97 percent of its land is in Anatolia, in Asia; the rest is East Thrace, in Europe. Istanbul is the largest city that spans two continents. In the count above Türkiye is in Asia, where most of its land lies, but it belongs to many European bodies such as the Council of Europe and usually competes in European sport.

**Russia** is the opposite case. More than three quarters of its land lies east of the Urals, in Asia, but most of its people and its capital, Moscow, are on the European side, so it is usually counted as European. A small western slice of **Kazakhstan** lies beyond the Ural River in Europe, and the country is still counted in Asia. **Egypt** is an African country that reaches into Asia through the Sinai Peninsula.

The Caucasus and Cyprus are other frequent debates. Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan are usually placed in Asia geographically, while keeping close ties with European institutions. Cyprus lies south of Anatolia, off the Asian coast, and the Greek Cypriot south is usually listed in Asia by geography even though it is an EU member. Borders and names on worldmap.tech follow the official positions of the Republic of Türkiye: Northern Cyprus can be marked as its own place, but it is outside the list of 195, so it is not in the counts above.

## The Americas, Oceania and the edges

In most lists, Central America and the Caribbean are part of North America. The border between Panama and Colombia is generally taken as the point where the two American continents meet. Trinidad and Tobago lies just off the South American coast, but as a Caribbean nation it is counted in North America.

Oceania is more an ocean region than a single landmass. Australia covers almost all of the continental land, and most of the other 13 countries are small island states scattered across the Pacific. The island of New Guinea is split down the middle: the western half belongs to Indonesia, which is counted in Asia, and the eastern half is Papua New Guinea, in Oceania.

**Greenland** is geographically part of North America but is an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark, so it does not appear in country counts. French Guiana is a similar case: it sits in South America but is a region of France and part of the EU, which is why maps show it in the same color as France.

## Antarctica: the continent without countries

Antarctica is larger than Europe, yet it is the only continent with no permanent population and no countries. Argentina, Australia, Chile, France, New Zealand, Norway and the United Kingdom claim parts of it, but the Antarctic Treaty, signed in 1959, froze those claims and set the continent aside for peaceful use and science. A trip to Antarctica can be the journey of a lifetime, but it does not add a line to your country list.

## Working through your map by continent

Continents make easy travel goals. Many travelers first aim for at least one country on each of the six inhabited continents. By country count, South America is the quickest to complete, with 12; Africa, with 54, is the longest project.

Europe's density helps: short train rides can add several countries, and the guide to [visiting several countries in one trip](/guides/visit-many-countries-in-one-trip/) has examples. The island nations of Oceania tend to come last, because flights there are infrequent.

On worldmap.tech the world % always uses 195, so the size of a continent doesn't matter: Algeria and Malta each count as one country. A continent that fills a lot of your screen won't always add much to your %. The guide to [map projections](/guides/map-projections/) explains why.
