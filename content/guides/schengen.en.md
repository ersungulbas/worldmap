The Schengen Area is a group of European countries that have dropped routine passport checks at their shared borders. As of November 2026 it has 29 members. Twenty-five of them are in the European Union and four are not: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Once you are inside, you can ride a train or bus from Paris to Prague and on to Budapest without stopping at a border check. For a travel map that is very handy: a single trip in Europe can easily add several countries.

## The 29 Schengen countries at a glance

On the map below, the blue countries are full Schengen members. Orange marks EU members outside Schengen, and purple the microstates that are not members but have open borders with Schengen countries.

**The 25 Schengen countries in the EU:** Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain and Sweden.

**The 4 Schengen countries outside the EU:** Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. They are members of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) and apply the Schengen rules through separate agreements.

## Schengen is not the same as the EU

The two lists overlap heavily but are not identical. Two of the EU's 27 members are not full Schengen members:

- **Ireland** chose to stay out so it could keep its separate Common Travel Area with the United Kingdom.
- **Cyprus** is preparing to join, but as of November 2026 no accession decision has been taken and border checks remain.

Meanwhile Norway, Switzerland, Iceland and Liechtenstein are inside Schengen without being in the EU. Europe's microstates each have their own arrangement. Monaco, San Marino and Vatican City are not Schengen members, but their borders with France and Italy are open, so in practice you enter them as if they were inside. Andorra is outside Schengen and has customs checks at its borders with Spain and France. If you travel on a single-entry Schengen visa, keep in mind that a trip to Andorra and back can use it up.

Schengen covers only the European territory of its members. Greenland and the Faroe Islands, both linked to Denmark, and France's regions outside Europe, such as French Guiana in South America, are not part of it.

## A short history

Schengen is named after a small village in Luxembourg on the Moselle, close to the German and French borders. Belgium, France, West Germany, Luxembourg and the Netherlands signed the agreement there on 14 June 1985. The convention that put it into practice followed in 1990, and the first checks were lifted on 26 March 1995. The area has grown step by step since then.

The latest enlargements are recent. Croatia joined on 1 January 2023. Bulgaria and Romania lifted checks at air and sea borders on 31 March 2024 and at land borders on 1 January 2025. That made both of Türkiye's western neighbors, Greece and Bulgaria, full Schengen members.

## What to know when you travel

Rules depend on your passport and change often, so treat the points below as a general frame. Before you travel, check the official website of the country you are visiting and your own government's travel advice.

**The 90/180 rule.** Travelers who are visa-exempt or hold a short-stay Schengen visa can stay up to 90 days in total within any 180-day period. The count is shared across all Schengen countries: days in Germany and days in Italy go into the same total.

**One visa, the whole area.** A short-stay Schengen visa is valid in all 29 countries. You usually apply to the country where you will spend the most time, or to the country you will enter first if the stays are equal.

**A new entry and exit system.** The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES) started on 12 October 2025 and has been fully running since 10 April 2026. Travelers from outside the EU have their fingerprints and a facial image recorded on first entry, and passports are no longer stamped. ETIAS, the travel authorization planned for visa-exempt visitors, has not started yet, and as of November 2026 no start date has been announced; the EU says it will give the date several months in advance. Travelers who need a visa will not need ETIAS.

**Temporary checks at internal borders.** Several member states have brought back temporary checks at some internal borders in recent years. Carry your passport or ID card even when crossing within Schengen.

## Schengen for your map

Schengen is one of the easiest ways to add many countries in a short time. Borders are often just a sign, intercity trains and buses are frequent, and distances are short.
