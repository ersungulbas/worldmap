The 10 Largest Countries in the World by Area

The world's ten largest countries cover roughly half of the planet's land. Russia, at the top of the list, alone covers more than 17 million km², about 70 percent more than the next country. On your map, though, each of these ten counts as a single country, just like Vatican City. This guide lists the ten largest, explains why the order shifts a little from one source to another, and what that means for your travel map.

The 10 largest countries by area

The figures are total area, including inland water such as lakes and rivers. They are rounded, and sources differ slightly depending on how they measure.

  • 1. Russia: about 17.1 million km². It stretches from Europe to the Pacific across 11 time zones.
  • 2. Canada: about 9.98 million km². It has the longest coastline in the world.
  • 3. United States: about 9.8 million km², including Alaska and Hawaii.
  • 4. China: about 9.6 million km². It has land borders with fourteen countries.
  • 5. Brazil: about 8.5 million km². Almost half of South America.
  • 6. Australia: about 7.7 million km². The only country that covers an entire continental landmass.
  • 7. India: about 3.3 million km². The most populous country in the world.
  • 8. Argentina: about 2.8 million km². The largest Spanish-speaking country.
  • 9. Kazakhstan: about 2.7 million km². The largest landlocked country.
  • 10. Algeria: about 2.4 million km². The largest country in Africa.

Is the US bigger than China?

Third and fourth place can swap depending on which figure you use. When the US total includes coastal and territorial waters, it comes out ahead of China; counting land alone, China is larger. Canada is a similar case: second by total area, but about a tenth of that area is lakes and rivers. Counting land only, the order becomes Russia, China, the United States and then Canada.

Differences like these explain why lists sometimes disagree. None of them is wrong; they are measuring slightly different things.

A few notes on the list

Russia covers roughly a ninth of the world's land. Most of its territory lies east of the Urals, in Asia, but most of its people and its capital, Moscow, are on the European side. The Trans-Siberian Railway from Moscow to Vladivostok is known as the longest railway line in the world.

Canada has the world's longest coastline once its thousands of islands are included. Most of the north is sparsely populated, and most Canadians live in a band close to the US border.

Brazil covers almost half of South America and borders every country on the continent except Chile and Ecuador.

Australia is both a country and the main landmass of a continent, which is counted as part of Oceania.

India became the world's most populous country in 2023, passing China according to UN estimates. Unlike most of the big countries on this list, it is densely populated.

Kazakhstan is the largest of the landlocked countries; it borders the Caspian Sea, but the Caspian does not connect to the ocean. The guide to landlocked countries covers them all.

Algeria has been the largest country in Africa since South Sudan separated from Sudan in 2011. Most of it is covered by the Sahara.

Then there are the ones that aren't on the list. Greenland, at about 2.17 million km², is the world's largest island, but it is an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark rather than a sovereign state. If it were independent, it would come just after Algeria and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, which is eleventh. Antarctica is larger than Europe but belongs to no country.

Big countries and your world %

Imagine visiting all ten: close to half the land on your map would turn blue. On worldmap.tech, though, the world % looks at the number of countries, not their size, and always uses 195. Ten countries are about 5% of the world, exactly the same as the ten smallest. For the other end of the scale, see the guide to the smallest countries in the world.

Sizes on the map can also mislead a little. Russia and Canada, far to the north, look somewhat bigger than they really are even on the Natural Earth projection the app uses; on projections like Mercator the effect is much stronger. The guide to map projections explains why.

To see which of the big ones you've covered, open the map, tap them and compare how much of the world turns blue with your %.
