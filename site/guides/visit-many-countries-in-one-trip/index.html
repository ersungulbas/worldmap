How to Visit Several Countries in One Trip

The easiest way to add several countries in one trip is to pick a region where the countries are small and the borders are close: Benelux, Central Europe, the Baltics, the Western Balkans, Europe's microstates, Georgia and Armenia, mainland Southeast Asia, Central America and the Gulf. Below is a sample route for each, how people usually get around, and one practical tip. Entry rules depend on your passport and change often, so before you book, check your foreign ministry's travel pages and each destination's official website.

Benelux and Central Europe

Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg

Route: Amsterdam → Antwerp → Brussels → Luxembourg City.

Trains do everything here. Direct services link Amsterdam and Brussels in about two hours, and InterCity trains run from Brussels to Luxembourg City roughly every hour, taking a little over three. Tip: public transport inside Luxembourg has been free for everyone since 2020, visitors included. Only first class still costs money.

Czechia, Austria, Slovakia and Hungary

Route: Prague → Vienna → Bratislava → Budapest.

All four capitals are linked by direct trains. Prague to Vienna takes just over four hours, Vienna and Bratislava are only about 55 km apart, roughly an hour by train, and direct trains continue from Bratislava to Budapest in around two and a half hours. Tip: if you're short on time, stay in Vienna and do Bratislava as a day trip.

The Baltics and the Western Balkans

Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania

Route: Tallinn → Riga → Vilnius, or the other way around. For a fourth country, start in Helsinki: ferries cross to Tallinn in about two to two and a half hours.

Long-distance buses connect the three capitals, and there's also a daily train with one easy change at Valga, on the border between Estonia and Latvia. Tip: the cross-border train is popular and can sell out, so book early.

The Western Balkans

Route: Dubrovnik (Croatia) → Kotor (Montenegro), then inland to Mostar and Sarajevo (Bosnia and Herzegovina), or south into Albania and on to North Macedonia.

Buses and minibuses do most of the work here. Dubrovnik to Kotor is a little over two hours by bus, plus whatever the border takes, and Sarajevo to Mostar has a scenic train ride of about two hours. Tip: border queues on the coast can be long in summer, so keep your passport in your hand luggage, not in the bag under the bus.

Europe's microstates

These five tiny countries are easy to fold into a bigger trip, and each counts as a full country on your map. Use the search box to find them, since they're too small to tap at world scale.

  • Vatican City is the smallest country in the world and sits inside Rome, so you just walk in.
  • San Marino has no train. Buses from Rimini take about an hour.
  • Monaco has its own underground station, just over 20 minutes by regional train from Nice.
  • Liechtenstein has no airport. Take a train to Sargans or Buchs in Switzerland, or Feldkirch in Austria, and switch to a local bus.
  • Andorra has no airport and no train station. Buses run from Barcelona and Toulouse.

Route idea: Rome and the Vatican, then a train toward the Adriatic coast for Rimini and San Marino. Or do the Riviera: Nice, Monaco and the Italian border town of Ventimiglia, under half an hour from Monaco by train.

Georgia and Armenia

Route: Tbilisi → Yerevan, with stops in Dilijan and at Lake Sevan if you travel with a driver. Then head back through Georgia or fly out of Yerevan.

Minibuses (marshrutkas) take about six hours including the border, private drivers stop where you like, and an overnight sleeper train links the two capitals. As of October 2026, Armenia's land borders with Azerbaijan and with Türkiye are closed, so you can't continue overland from Armenia into either country. Reopening the Türkiye border is under discussion, so check the latest news. Tip: the sleeper train runs on alternate nights rather than every day, so look at its calendar before you fix your dates.

Southeast Asia, Central America and the Gulf

Thailand, Laos, Vietnam and Cambodia

Route: Bangkok → Vientiane → Luang Prabang → Hanoi → south through Vietnam → Phnom Penh → Siem Reap.

A direct sleeper train has run from Bangkok to Vientiane since 2024, and fast trains link Vientiane and Luang Prabang in about two hours. From Laos you can fly to Hanoi or take a long overnight bus, and buses connect Ho Chi Minh City with Phnom Penh. Tip: as of October 2026, the land crossings between Thailand and Cambodia are closed, which is why this route avoids that border. If you want Bangkok and Angkor on the same trip, plan to fly that leg.

Central America

Route: Guatemala → El Salvador → Honduras → Nicaragua → Costa Rica → Panama. For something shorter, pair Belize with Guatemala: shuttles from San Ignacio reach Tikal or Flores in about two hours.

Tourist shuttles and long-distance coaches link the main towns, and Tica Bus alone connects six countries in the region. Tip: there's no road between Panama and Colombia because of the Darién Gap, so plan to fly if you're continuing to South America. Read your government's travel advice for each country too, since conditions vary.

The Gulf

Route: Dubai → Muscat by bus, a flight to Doha, then Bahrain and across the King Fahd Causeway into eastern Saudi Arabia.

Coaches run between the UAE and Oman, with Dubai to Muscat taking about six to seven hours depending on the border. The 25 km causeway links Bahrain and Saudi Arabia, and short flights cover the rest. Tip: if you rent a car, ask the company before booking whether you can take it across a border.

Before you book

More borders means more things to check:

  • Entry rules: they depend on your passport and can change at short notice. Check your foreign ministry and each destination's official website before you travel, and again close to departure.
  • Border status: crossings can close or reopen quickly, as the examples above show.
  • Timetables: cross-border trains and buses change with the seasons, so confirm with the operator.
  • Your counting rule: decide in advance whether a day trip or a border town counts. Our guide on what counts as visiting a country can help.

When you're back, tap the new countries on the map and see how far your count and your traveler level have moved.
