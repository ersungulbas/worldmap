Türkiye's Neighbors: Eight Land Borders on One Map

Türkiye has eight land neighbors. Greece and Bulgaria are in Thrace, Georgia is to the northeast, Armenia, Azerbaijan (through Nakhchivan) and Iran are to the east, and Iraq and Syria are to the south. In the Mediterranean, Northern Cyprus has no land border with Türkiye but is one of its closest neighbors. This guide places each one on the map with its main border crossings, and covers the neighbors by sea.

The borders at a glance

Türkiye spans two continents: about 3 percent of its land is in East Thrace, in Europe, and the rest is Anatolia, in Asia. Its neighbors are European and Asian too. The longest land border, at roughly 900 kilometers, is with Syria; the shortest, with Azerbaijan's Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic, is under 20 kilometers. Sources give slightly different lengths, so the ranking is more useful to remember than exact figures.

Here are the neighbors and their main crossings in short:

  • Greece: İpsala and Pazarkule, both in Edirne. The Evros (Meriç) river forms most of the border.
  • Bulgaria: Kapıkule near Edirne, one of Europe's busiest land crossings, plus Dereköy and Hamzabeyli in Kırklareli.
  • Georgia: Sarp on the Black Sea coast in Artvin, and Türkgözü and Aktaş in Ardahan.
  • Armenia: the land border has been closed since 1993.
  • Azerbaijan (Nakhchivan): the Dilucu crossing in Iğdır.
  • Iran: Gürbulak in Ağrı, Kapıköy in Van and Esendere in Hakkari.
  • Iraq: Habur in Şırnak, the most used crossing.
  • Syria: crossings such as Cilvegözü in Hatay and Öncüpınar in Kilis, which may open or close depending on security.

To the west: Greece and Bulgaria

Türkiye's two European neighbors are both members of the EU and the Schengen Area. Bulgaria became a full Schengen member when it lifted land border checks on 1 January 2025. Crossing from Edirne into Bulgaria or Greece therefore also means entering Schengen; the entry rules are summed up in the guide to the Schengen Area.

Some Greek islands in the Aegean lie only a few kilometers off the Turkish coast. Lesbos faces Ayvalık, Chios faces Çeşme, Samos faces Kuşadası and Kastellorizo sits right off Kaş. Ferries to these islands can be a more enjoyable way to add Greece than a land border.

To the northeast: Georgia

Georgia is one of the easiest neighbors to reach from Türkiye. The Black Sea coast road runs past Hopa to the Sarp crossing, and Batumi is a short ride from the other side. From Georgia you can continue into Armenia and see two Caucasus countries on one trip; there is a sample route in the guide to visiting several countries in one trip.

To the east: Armenia, Nakhchivan and Iran

The land border with Armenia has been closed since 1993 and was still closed as of October 2026. Reopening it has been on the agenda for some time, but for now reaching Armenia from Türkiye means going around through Georgia or flying.

Türkiye's border with Azerbaijan runs only along Nakhchivan, a part of Azerbaijan wedged between Armenia, Iran and Türkiye, with no direct land link to the rest of the country. Crossing at Dilucu in Iğdır adds Azerbaijan to your map, but continuing overland to Baku means a detour through Iran.

The border with Iran runs south from the foot of Mount Ararat (Ağrı Dağı), at 5,137 meters the highest mountain in Türkiye. Gürbulak, near Doğubayazıt, is the crossing most used by buses and trucks heading into Iran.

To the south: Iraq and Syria

Türkiye's longest land borders are in the south. The border with Iraq is much shorter than the one with Syria; the Habur crossing leads into northern Iraq. The border with Syria runs from Hatay all the way to the Iraqi border, and part of it follows the line of the old Baghdad Railway.

Travel conditions in both countries can change quickly. Before you go, check your government's current travel advice and the status of the crossings.

Neighbors by sea, and Northern Cyprus

Beyond its land borders, Türkiye shares three seas with other countries. Across the Black Sea are Bulgaria, Romania, Ukraine, Russia and Georgia. In the Aegean there is Greece with its islands, and in the Mediterranean the island of Cyprus.

Cyprus lies just off Türkiye's south coast. Türkiye recognizes the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (KKTC) in the north of the island, and you can mark it as a separate place on the map. Turkish citizens can travel there with a national ID card, and ferries run from Taşucu to Girne (Kyrenia). The south of the island is the Greek Cypriot administration. Borders and names on the map follow the official positions of the Republic of Türkiye.

Neighbors alone can take your map to 10

A nice coincidence: Türkiye, its eight land neighbors and Northern Cyprus add up to exactly 10. On worldmap.tech that is the Explorer level. Because the % uses 195, those 10 places are about 5% of the world; the guide to traveler levels explains the math.

Not every neighbor is equally easy to visit, but most are a few hours by bus or a short flight away. Crossing from Edirne into Bulgaria and Greece, from Artvin into Georgia or from Iğdır into Nakhchivan can even fit into a weekend.
