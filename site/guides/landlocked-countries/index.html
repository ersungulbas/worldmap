Landlocked Countries: All 44 on One Map

Of the world's 195 countries, 44 have no coastline. That is a little over a fifth of all countries. The list includes giants like Kazakhstan and countries that fit inside a single city, like Vatican City. This guide sorts all 44 by continent, shows them on the map and explains why the number changes a little from one source to another.

The 44 countries by continent

The count uses the list of 195: the 193 UN member states plus the observer states Vatican City and Palestine.

  • Africa (16): Botswana, Burkina Faso, Burundi, the Central African Republic, Chad, Eswatini, Ethiopia, Lesotho, Malawi, Mali, Niger, Rwanda, South Sudan, Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe.
  • Europe (14): Andorra, Austria, Belarus, Czechia, Hungary, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Moldova, North Macedonia, San Marino, Serbia, Slovakia, Switzerland and Vatican City.
  • Asia (12): Afghanistan, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Bhutan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Laos, Mongolia, Nepal, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan.
  • South America (2): Bolivia and Paraguay.

North America and Oceania have no landlocked countries; most of Oceania's countries are islands anyway.

Why the number varies

The biggest debate is about the Caspian Sea. Despite its name, the Caspian is the world's largest enclosed body of water and has no link to the ocean. This list counts Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan, which border the Caspian, as landlocked, as sources such as Britannica do. Lists that treat the Caspian as a sea leave those three out, which brings the total to 41. A convention signed by the five Caspian countries in 2018 gives it a special status that is neither a sea nor a lake, so even the legal answer is not clear-cut.

The other difference is which list of countries you start from. Kosovo is landlocked too, but it is not among the 195, so it is not in the 44. On worldmap.tech you can still mark Kosovo as its own place.

The biggest and the most populous

Kazakhstan, at about 2.7 million km², is the largest landlocked country and the ninth-largest country in the world. Mongolia comes next at about 1.56 million km², and it is also the most sparsely populated country in the world.

Ethiopia, with more than a hundred million people, is the most populous landlocked country. It lost its coast when Eritrea became independent in 1993.

Bolivia once had a Pacific coast too. It lost its coastal region to Chile in the War of the Pacific, fought from 1879 to 1884. Bolivia still keeps a navy that operates on Lake Titicaca and the rivers, and it marks 23 March every year as the Day of the Sea.

The two doubly landlocked countries

Some countries are surrounded only by other landlocked countries, so reaching the sea means crossing at least two borders. There are two in the world:

Liechtenstein sits between Switzerland and Austria, neither of which has a coast.

Uzbekistan is surrounded by Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan and Turkmenistan. This one depends on the Caspian question too: if you count the Caspian as a sea, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan become coastal countries and Uzbekistan drops off this list.

Surrounded by a single country

Three countries are completely surrounded by just one other country: Vatican City and San Marino by Italy, and Lesotho by South Africa. Lesotho has another distinction: all of its territory lies more than 1,000 meters above sea level, and it is the only country that can say so.

Landlocked countries on your travel map

In Europe, most of these countries are easy to reach. Switzerland, Austria, Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary are linked by trains, and Vienna, Bratislava and Budapest fit into the same week; the guide to visiting several countries in one trip has the details. Hungarians even call Lake Balaton the "Hungarian Sea", a good example of what a missing coast means.

Two of Türkiye's neighbors, Armenia and Azerbaijan, are on the list too, so a trip around the Caucasus can add two landlocked countries at once; the guide to Türkiye's neighbors maps them out. Central Asia and the interior of Africa are the harder part of the list, because flights there are less frequent.
